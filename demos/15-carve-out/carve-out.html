What the ledger actually looks like

Most of the P&L segments itself: revenue and direct costs post to a division cost centre and need no judgement at all. The carve-out problem is the remainder — the cost sitting in shared and corporate cost centres that carries no division tag, and never has.

Allocating the shared functions

The five shared services do not only serve the divisions — they serve each other. IT runs the finance and payroll systems; HR runs payroll for IT. A single pass down to the divisions gets that wrong, which is why the reciprocal method exists.

The reciprocal solve

Each pool's true cost is its own spend plus what it receives from the others, which is circular and therefore a linear system rather than a sequence:

T = D + M·T M[i][j] = share of service j consumed by i ⇒ T = (I − M)⁻¹ D

      About this data

      Ashworth Industries is synthetic. The generator emits what a carve-out team actually receives: posting-level journal lines across cost centres, a service usage matrix, headcount and floor area, and the sub-ledgers behind receivables, inventory, fixed assets, payables and accruals. Nothing is pre-segmented.

      Every reconciliation is asserted in the build: all three allocation methods conserve the pool exactly, corporate spreads completely on every basis, the carve-out bridge ties to , and each balance sheet line foots across the three divisions to the group total.
