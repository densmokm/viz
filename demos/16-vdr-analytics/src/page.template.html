Who is actually working the deal

Engagement measures effort invested — coverage, dwell time, seniority of the people reading, adviser firms onboarded, questions asked — scored against the same measures taken from 140 bidders in past processes. Withdrawal risk comes from a model fitted on those outcomes.

The drop-out model

Fitted by logistic regression on prior processes, not hand-weighted. The coefficients are what the history says, and they are worth reading on their own: going quiet is the loudest signal, and onboarding advisers is close behind.

    Method notes

      About this data

      Thornbury Precision is synthetic. The generator models six bidders with distinct attention profiles, team structures and activity paths, a document set with a realistic long tail of material nobody opens, and a handful of documents carrying the issues that end up in the SPA — unlabelled, so the heat scoring has to find them. It emits access events. The withdrawal model is fitted on a separate register of 140 bidders from prior processes.

      A caution worth stating. This is behavioural inference, not fact. A quiet bidder may be waiting on an investment committee; a bidder reading only customer contracts may have a thesis rather than an ulterior motive. The output is a ranked set of questions for the deal team, not a verdict on anyone's intent.
